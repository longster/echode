How a city opens

No launch dates and no hype. A city grows here the way communities actually grow, one listing and one gathering at a time. That is how this works: Echode is the map, and each city keeps its own local home, run the local way. Houston's home is HoustonDeafNetwork, and its address here, echode.com/htx, always points to it.

1

It starts with people

Someone local suggests a Deaf-owned business, a helpful service, a resource worth knowing. There is no magic number, but around ten solid listings is usually when a page starts to feel alive instead of empty.

2

Things keep happening

Meetups, interpreted shows, game nights, church gatherings. When things happen more than once, the city has a pulse, and people have a reason to keep coming back.

3

The page opens

The city gets its own page here and its own short address, like echode.com/htx for Houston. The address never changes, even if the local site behind it does someday.

What is Echode?

Echo + code. Think of an echo as a signal. You send one out... a hand waving to get someone's attention, a light flashing, a post, a sign... and something comes back: a reply, a connection, a community answering. That back and forth is how Deaf community life already works: visual, direct, person to person. Code is how we build the thing that carries the signal further. Echode started as a small company name years ago; today it is becoming the umbrella for local Deaf community networks across Texas.

Our objective is simple: connect communities. Locally first, because community life happens in your own city... your coffee chat, your gym, your church, your kid's school. And statewide over time, because when you travel, move, or visit family across Texas, your community should already be mapped when you get there. Every listing, event, and city page here exists for that one reason: helping people find each other.

HoustonDeafNetwork is the first city, and it is not moving or changing. It stays exactly where it is, and Echode simply points to it. New cities get the same treatment: local first, always.

One more thing, so there is no confusion about who is behind this: Echode is a personal project. I built it and I keep it running... Long Duong(opens Long Duong's homepage in a new tab), the same person behind HoustonDeafNetwork. It is not a company and not a nonprofit, and it is honestly not about me. The communities are the point. If you want to help your own city grow here, my arms are open. I will share what I have learned, help you get started, and point people your way, any way I can. The only line I hold is keeping this true to its core: local first, honest, and free for the community it serves.

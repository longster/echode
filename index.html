How new cities join

No launch dates and no hype. A city grows here the way communities actually grow, one listing and one gathering at a time. That is how this works: Echode is the map, and each city keeps its own local home, run the local way. Houston's home is HoustonDeafNetwork, and its address here, echode.com/htx, always points to it.

1

It starts with people

Someone local suggests a Deaf-owned business, a helpful service, a resource worth knowing. There is no magic number, but around ten solid listings is usually when a page starts to feel alive instead of empty.

2

Things keep happening

Meetups, interpreted shows, game nights, church gatherings. When things happen more than once, the city has a pulse, and people have a reason to keep coming back.

3

The city goes live

The city gets its own page here and its own short address, like echode.com/satx for San Antonio.

What is Echode?

ECHO is the signal. DE is the Deaf community. Think of an echo as a signal. You send one out... a hand waving to get someone's attention, a light flashing, a post, a sign... and something comes back: a reply, a connection, a community answering. That back and forth is how Deaf community life already works: visual, direct, person to person. Echode started as a small company name years ago; today it is becoming the umbrella for local Deaf community networks across Texas.

  • One front door

    echode.com, with short addresses like /htx and /atx that never change.

  • Local first

    Community life happens in your city. Texas-wide comes next.

  • Houston stays put

    Live now at HoustonDeafNetwork.com. Not moving, not changing.

  • With local groups

    Built to partner with Facebook groups, not replace them.

  • Free for the community

    The community side stays free. Paid placements are always labeled.

  • Run lean, built to last

    No bloat, no noise. Just what the community needs.

One more thing, so there is no confusion about who is behind this: Echode is a personal project. I built it and I keep it running, and it is honestly not about me. The communities are the point. If you want to help your city grow here, my arms are open.

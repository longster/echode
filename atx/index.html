New to Austin's Deaf community? Start here

Three good first doors: Austin Deaf Club for a standing social hub, Deaf Night Out ATX (every 3rd Saturday, venue announced in its Facebook group) for a low pressure night out, and the #OurDeafCommunity: Austin Facebook group, where most of the city's Deaf events and announcements get posted. Into sports? Austin Deaf Pickleball runs leagues and beginner friendly socials.

How to find an ASL interpreter in Austin

For medical, legal, work, or school interpreting, start with a local agency: Communication By Hand (24/7, since 1993), 3 Bridges Sign Language Services, or Texas Sign Language Interpreter Services, all in the directory above. For theatre and arts events, Ploeger ASL Interpreting specializes in exactly that. Book as early as you can, and ask for BEI certified interpreters when the setting is formal... Texas certifies interpreters through the state HHSC Deaf and Hard of Hearing Services office, also listed above.

Where to learn ASL in Austin

For structured classes, Austin Community College offers ASL I through VI plus an interpreter training degree, and UT Austin offers ASL courses and an ASL Studies minor. Texas School for the Deaf also hosts community ASL classes on its campus. For practice, the Austin American Sign Language Social group on Facebook runs voice off socials for signers of all levels.

FAQ

Is Echode Austin open?

Not officially. Austin is in the collecting stage: seeded listings are in, and the page opens fully once the community starts adding to it and events recur. Houston is the live example of what an open city looks like.

How do I get listed?

Use the Get Listed section below. Every submission goes to a review queue first, and community listings are always free. Businesses get the same review; paid visibility, when it exists, is always labeled.

Who runs this?

Echode is a one person community project run by Long Duong in Houston, the same person behind HoustonDeafNetwork. Not a nonprofit, no office, just a directory built to last.
